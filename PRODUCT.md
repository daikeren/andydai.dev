# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

One primary reader, backed by two technical ones:

- **Primary: managers leading AI adoption inside Taiwanese companies.** They have already decided to adopt AI. What they need is *how*: where to start, how to scale without stalling, which risks are real, and how to keep quality from collapsing once everyone has access. They care less about multi-agent architectures or eval harnesses, and more about decisions they can take to their team and their boss. LinkedIn (in Chinese) is where they usually find the writing.
- **Peers and senior practitioners** — engineers, tech leads, and founders who want hard-won, in-the-weeds lessons on AI engineering, developer productivity, team management, and running a startup. They read long-form, often on a laptop or phone, and they can tell the difference between firsthand experience and recycled hype.
- **People evaluating Andy** — recruiters, potential collaborators, and investors who land here to gauge his expertise, judgement, and taste. For them the blog is evidence, not entertainment.

Content is bilingual (Traditional Chinese primary, English secondary) with heavy inline English technical vocabulary woven into Chinese prose. The audience is comfortable code-switching.

## Product Purpose

A personal blog (andydai.dev, "Andy's System") where Andy Dai shares firsthand experience in AI engineering, startups, and technical leadership. It exists to build a credible, trusted voice — reputation is the goal, not pageviews. Success looks like the right people (peers, collaborators, hiring managers) reading a post, trusting the reasoning, and remembering who wrote it. Every post should make Andy more credible, not just more visible.

## Positioning & Content Pillars

Whether to adopt AI is no longer the question. The question is **how to adopt AI well, and what to watch out for**. Andy's credibility on that comes from building with AI every day (Codeer, a six-person team shipping an AI agent platform), so the engineering posts are the evidence, and enterprise adoption is the subject.

Most posts should land in one of these pillars, each drawn from a pitfall Andy keeps seeing in Taiwanese companies:

- **A. Start small, then scale.** Teams stall while debating how to roll out at scale and drown in details. Scale comes from running something small and verifiable, not from planning it upfront.
- **B. Security: separate real risks from imagined ones.** Fear of security issues blocks adoption. Break vague fear into concrete risk layers: which ones need controls, and which ones are imagined. (No posts yet. This is the biggest gap.)
- **C. Mindless use produces garbage.** Handing everyone a license turns cheap output into expensive judgment. Adoption needs verification and feedback loops built in.
- **D. Practitioner proof.** How Andy's own team actually builds with AI. This pillar supports A–C rather than standing alone.

When writing for the primary reader, keep the same rigor, but translate the engineering lesson into the decision a manager has to make.

## Brand Personality

**Rigorous, candid, practitioner.** The voice is opinionated and direct — it cuts through hype and says the real thing — but authority comes from disciplined argument, not from being punchy. Posts build careful distinctions (e.g. separating impression / regression / behavior change / motive), ground claims in firsthand startup operation ("我在台灣經營一間 AI startup"), and hold themselves to a higher evidentiary bar than the takes they're rebutting. Calm and measured, never breathless. Anti-hype, pro-evidence, generous with hard-won detail. The design should match: confident and reading-first, quietly distinctive rather than loud.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI / SaaS template.** No gradient hero, no feature-card grids, no tiny tracked uppercase eyebrows, no "startup landing page" scaffolding. The blog must never look like the thing its posts critique.
- **Cluttered / busy.** No sidebars stuffed with widgets, no popups, no ad-style density, nothing that competes with the writing for attention.
- **Trendy / gimmicky.** No heavy decorative animation, glassmorphism, or neon effects that age badly and distract from reading.

## Design Principles

1. **The writing is the product.** Every design decision serves reading comfort and focus first. Typography, measure, and rhythm matter more than ornament.
2. **Quietly distinctive.** Reading-first, but unmistakably Andy's — a few deliberate, characterful touches (the yellow highlighter underline is the existing signature) rather than a neutral default theme.
3. **Practice what you preach.** The site critiques hype and lazy AI aesthetics; it must hold itself to that same standard. No slop, no template smell.
4. **Evidence over decoration.** Visual emphasis should track real meaning (a key distinction, a pull quote, a TL;DR), the way the prose earns its claims. Don't decorate what isn't important.
5. **Bilingual by design.** Chinese and English sit side by side in the same sentence; type, spacing, and line length must stay legible and balanced across both scripts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Body text must clear 4.5:1 contrast in both light and dark modes (the palette is near-grayscale on a tinted-neutral background, so verify the secondary text color holds up). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for the highlight, zoom, and transition effects. Maintain comfortable line length and CJK-aware line-height for long-form bilingual reading. Honor the existing light / dark / auto theme switching.
